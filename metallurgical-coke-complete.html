CARBON MATERIAL

Metallurgical Coke

Metallurgical coke, commonly known as met coke, is a carbon-rich material produced by heating suitable coking coal in the absence of oxygen. It plays a critical role in ironmaking and other high-temperature metallurgical processes.

In blast furnace ironmaking, coke performs several functions at the same time. It acts as a source of carbon, contributes heat through combustion, supports the furnace burden at high temperature and helps maintain permeability for gases moving through the furnace.

At Kailash Coal & Coke Co. Ltd. (KCCL), we support customers with metallurgical coke sourcing based on application, size requirement and quality parameters such as ash, moisture, volatile matter, sulphur, CRI and CSR.

Because coke quality requirements differ by furnace and process, KCCL focuses on requirement-based sourcing rather than presenting a single specification as suitable for every customer.

PRODUCT CHARACTERISTICS

Key Characteristics

Effective metallurgical coke is evaluated not only by chemistry, but also by its mechanical strength and behaviour under the high-temperature, reactive conditions of a furnace.

High Carbon Content

Coke provides carbon required for reduction reactions and high-temperature metallurgical processing.

Mechanical Strength

Strong coke helps support the furnace burden without excessive breakdown during handling and furnace descent.

Coke Strength After Reaction

CSR is an important indicator of how well coke retains strength after reacting with carbon dioxide.

Controlled Reactivity

CRI is used to assess coke reactivity under conditions relevant to blast-furnace operation.

Size Consistency

Appropriate and consistent sizing supports permeability, handling and stable furnace operation.

Controlled Impurities

Ash, sulphur, moisture and other chemistry parameters are reviewed against end-use requirements.

APPLICATIONS

Where Metallurgical Coke Is Used

Blast Furnaces Used as fuel, reducing agent and structural support material during hot-metal production.
Iron & Steel Plants A principal raw material in integrated iron and steelmaking operations.
Foundries Suitable coke grades can be used in cupola furnaces and other foundry applications.
Ferroalloys Carbon materials are used in selected ferroalloy and high-temperature reduction processes.
Mineral Processing Coke can serve as a carbon source in selected metallurgical reduction processes.
Industrial Carbon Use Different coke sizes and grades can serve specialist high-temperature industrial requirements.

QUALITY PARAMETERS

Understanding Met Coke Quality

Metallurgical coke specifications vary by furnace, application, supplier and cargo. These are the principal quality parameters commonly reviewed during technical and commercial evaluation.

Parameter What It Indicates Why It Matters Evaluation Focus
Fixed Carbon The carbon-rich combustible portion remaining after accounting for moisture, ash and volatile matter. Important for heat generation and carbon availability during metallurgical reactions. Evaluated against the required metallurgical process and overall coke chemistry.
Ash Non-combustible mineral matter remaining after combustion. Higher ash contributes additional slag load and can affect furnace efficiency and coke value. Usually controlled according to furnace and process limits.
Total Sulphur Sulphur present in the coke. Sulphur can transfer into hot metal and influence downstream steelmaking requirements. Reviewed closely for ironmaking and metallurgical applications.
Moisture Water contained in the coke at the time of analysis or delivery. Affects delivered weight, handling and effective usable material. Controlled commercially and operationally by contract.
Volatile Matter Volatile compounds released when coke is heated under specified conditions. Helps indicate the degree of carbonisation and coke quality. Typically maintained at a low level for well-carbonised met coke.
CRI Coke Reactivity Index, measuring reaction with carbon dioxide under a standard test. Indicates how readily coke reacts under blast-furnace-like conditions. Assessed together with CSR rather than in isolation.
CSR Coke Strength After Reaction, measuring the strength retained after the CRI reaction test. Indicates the ability of coke to remain physically strong after reacting in high-temperature furnace conditions. Higher required strength depends on furnace design and operation.
Size Distribution The physical particle-size range of the supplied coke. Consistent size supports furnace permeability, handling and predictable process behaviour. Final size range should be matched to the customer's application.

Important: This table explains quality parameters only. It does not represent guaranteed KCCL specifications. Final chemistry, CSR, CRI, sizing and other limits should be confirmed against the specific product, supplier certificate and purchase contract.

BLAST FURNACE PERFORMANCE

Why Coke Is Critical in Ironmaking

Inside a blast furnace, coke performs more than one job. Its thermal, chemical and physical functions work together to support stable iron production.

01

Source of Heat

Coke combusts with the hot blast and helps generate the high temperatures required inside the furnace.

02

Reducing Agent

Carbon in coke contributes to the reducing gases and reactions that convert iron oxides into metallic iron.

03

Burden Support

Strong coke helps physically support iron-bearing materials as they descend through the furnace.

04

Gas Permeability

Coke creates void space that helps hot reducing gases move through the packed furnace burden.
